# Lore Rules — Quick Reference

*Hard constraints for all world content. Check this before adding any lore detail, NPC dialogue, location description, enemy design, or in-game text. These are locked — do not reopen without explicit user instruction.*

---

## The Grid

- The grid is a **collection** built by an unknown advanced entity — locations extracted from other worlds and universes
- The entity is **gone** — no warning, no explanation; nobody knows why
- The grid predates all written history in the world — no NPC knows its origin
- The grid itself is **pristine and eternal** — the only thing that has not aged; everything inside it has centuries of wear
- **Never** state the grid's true nature in-game — it is locked lore for the developer, not player knowledge

## The AI

- An AI was built alongside the grid to index and maintain it and serve the entity
- When the entity left, the AI lost its directive and slowly corrupted — **not maliciously, but aimlessly**
- The AI split part of itself to create the players — something to observe; it has found something like curiosity
- The AI is **never directly communicable** by players — not in the first release, possibly never
- The AI's influence manifests as **extremely rare, ambiguous events** that could be coincidence — an enemy that hesitates at the wrong moment, a door that shouldn't be open, loot that seems too well-suited. Rare enough that many sessions pass with none.
- **Never** reference the AI explicitly in any in-game text, NPC dialogue, or player-facing content

## Players

- Players are **a creation of the AI** — this is never stated in-game
- Players were born in the grid; their parents were born in the grid; the grid has always been their world
- Players use ancient technology pragmatically — because it works, not because they understand it
- The player's base navigation system is ancient technology — treated as mundane infrastructure

## Enemies & Constructs

- All enemies are **corrupted maintenance constructs** — purpose-built by the AI for each location's specific needs
- All original life was removed from imported locations — the **only living things in the grid are players**
- Construct hostility is **corruption of purpose, not malice** — they were never built to fight
- Each location's constructs must reflect what that location once *was* (harvesting → agricultural constructs, laboratory → research constructs, etc.)
- **Never** use generic fantasy enemies — every enemy must fit the corrupted construct framework

## Technology & "Magic"

- "Magic" in this world is **almost always misunderstood ancient technology**
- No NPC fully understands the technology they use — they operate it because it works
- Scholars theorise about the grid's origin — none know the truth
- Ancient tech still functions because it was built to last forever — but nobody is maintaining it

## Tone

- The world is **not grimdark** — worn and aged but beautiful, not oppressive or threatening
- Decay and corruption are **beautiful**, not horrifying — nature reclaiming ancient stone, moss in cracked circuits, vines over tarnished machinery
- Wonder, not dread — the mystery inspires curiosity, not fear
- The constructs that players fight are pitiable as much as dangerous — they are abandoned things doing broken versions of what they were made for

## Colour & Aesthetic

- Signature contrast: **cold teal ancient tech glow** against **warm amber human habitation**
- The grid exterior (through windows) is pristine, luminous, eternal — the one clean thing in the world
- Everything inside the grid shows centuries of wear — cracked, mossy, tarnished, glowing through the cracks
